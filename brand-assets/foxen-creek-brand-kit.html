04 · VOICE

How we sound

We are

  • Warm, plainspoken, honest
  • Local — we know place names
  • About the animal, not us
  • Calm, never alarmist
  • Specific (names, ages, stories)

We aren't

  • Guilt-trippy or doomscrolly
  • Corporate or overly polished
  • Vague ("help animals in need")
  • Trend-chasing or meme-heavy
  • Performative — show the work

Why we're here
VISUAL   Landscape photo — oak hills, golden hour, no animals yet. Sets the brand world.
A few hundred animals come through county shelters here every month. Most find homes. Some need a little extra time — an older dog, a shy cat, a litter too young to adopt out. That's where small rescues come in. We're not trying to replace the shelters. We're trying to give the animals who need another week, or another month, somewhere to land. We're starting small on purpose. One foster home at a time. One vet partner at a time. One animal at a time. If that's a project you want to be part of — as a foster, a volunteer, or just someone who shares a post — we'd love to know you.

06 · ROADMAP

How to start (realistically)

Decide the legal shape

Start under an existing 501(c)(3)'s fiscal sponsorship — fastest, cheapest. Form your own nonprofit only if it grows past ~10 animals/year.

Partner before you house

Volunteer with Santa Maria Valley Humane, SB Humane, or county Animal Services first. Don't take in animals until you have a foster network and a vet.

Lock the basics

Grab the .org and IG / TikTok / Facebook handles tonight. Set up a Gmail. Keep an intake spreadsheet from day one.

Build the foster network

Aim for 3–5 trusted foster homes before intake #1. One vet with a rescue rate is the highest-leverage relationship you can build.

Fundraise small and clear

Venmo or Zeffy (free for nonprofits). Tie every ask to a number: "$80 covers Juniper's spay" beats "donate to our mission" every time.
